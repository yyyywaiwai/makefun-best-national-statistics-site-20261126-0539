Prices & inflation

After decades of near-zero inflation, Japan's consumer prices have risen more than 2% a year since 2022. Food is the pressure point: rice prices roughly doubled in 2025 after a poor harvest and tight supply.

The break with deflation. Japan spent the 2010s with inflation near zero. The 2022–2023 surge came from energy and import costs after the yen fell; by 2024–2025 the driver had shifted to food and services, with wages finally rising to match.

How to read the CPI

The Consumer Price Index tracks the prices of about 580 goods and services bought by households, weighted by what people actually spend. Core CPI leaves out fresh food, whose prices swing with the weather. Core-core also leaves out energy. The Bank of Japan targets 2% on the core-core measure, and watches it together with wages: inflation only sticks if pay rises with it. That is why the 2025 wage round — the biggest in 34 years — matters as much as any price index on this page. See Labour for the wage figures.
