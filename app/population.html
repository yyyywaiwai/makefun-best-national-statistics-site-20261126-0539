Population & demographics

Japan's population peaked at 128.1 million in 2008 and has fallen every year since. The story is not just the total — it is who is left: fewer children, more people over 75.

Births and deaths, 2024

Source: Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare, Vital Statistics 2024.

Natural decrease of 884,310 people in 2024. Births fell to 720,988 — the lowest since records began — while deaths reached 1,605,298. Net migration (about +340,000) offsets only part of the gap.

What the numbers mean

Japan is the world's oldest large country and its fastest-shrinking one among major economies. The working-age population has fallen from 87 million in 1995 to under 74 million today, which is why labour shortages, pension costs and regional depopulation dominate policy debate. The prefecture picture is uneven: Tokyo still grows slightly, while Akita loses over 1% of its people a year — see the Regions page.
