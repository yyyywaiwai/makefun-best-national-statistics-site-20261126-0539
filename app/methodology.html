Sources & method

Every figure on this site comes from an official Japanese statistical release. This page lists them, explains how figures were chosen, and states the limits honestly.

Sources used

  • Population EstimatesStatistics Bureau of Japan
  • Population Census 2020Statistics Bureau of Japan
  • National AccountsCabinet Office
  • Prefectural AccountsCabinet Office
  • Labour Force SurveyStatistics Bureau of Japan
  • Monthly Labour SurveyMinistry of Health, Labour and Welfare
  • Vital StatisticsMinistry of Health, Labour and Welfare
  • Consumer Price IndexStatistics Bureau of Japan
  • Trade StatisticsMinistry of Finance
  • Policy rateBank of Japan

Figures are the latest published values as of November 2026. Where a 2025 figure is an estimate rather than a final release, the chart says so.

How figures were chosen

  • Reference yearShown on every chart
  • Rounding1 decimal, display only
  • RevisionsLatest vintage used
  • Seasonal adjustmentAnnual averages used
  • CurrencyYen; $ at ≈¥152/$
  • Prefecture GDP2022, latest available

Independence note. This atlas is not produced by, affiliated with, or endorsed by any government agency. It quotes official figures and names the source each time, so any reader can check the original release.

Definitions

  • Total populationAll residents of Japan on 1 October, including foreign residents staying 3+ months
  • Nominal GDPGDP at current prices, not adjusted for inflation
  • Real GDP growthChange in GDP at constant prices, year on year
  • Unemployment rateUnemployed as a share of the labour force, seasonally adjusted annual average
  • CPI inflationChange in the all-items Consumer Price Index, 2020 base = 100
  • Labour forceEveryone aged 15+ who is employed or actively seeking work
  • Fertility rateAverage children a woman would bear over her lifetime at current age-specific rates

Limits and caveats

Prefectural unemployment rates are model-based estimates and carry wider error than the national figure. Prefectural GDP per capita is only published with a two-year lag, so the table uses 2022. The 2025 figures on this site are the most recent published estimates and will be revised as final data arrive — the overview's year selector lets you compare any year from 2019 onward. Trade and GDP figures are rounded; small components may not sum exactly to totals.

Corrections: if you spot a figure that does not match its named source, it is an error in this atlas, not in the source. The site is a static, self-contained build — it loads nothing from any other origin.
